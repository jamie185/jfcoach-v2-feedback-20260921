01

Nutrition, made easier

A warmer green and coral treatment, readable photo cards, full recipe previews and a direct route from this week’s recipe challenge.

  • The weekly recipe challenge opens My Meal Guide and explains the + → Nutrition route for next time.
  • Recipe titles sit beside photographs and wrap fully. Popular photographed meals rank more prominently.
  • Fourteen everyday recipes add chicken, quick family meals, curries and dairy-free meat options. Supermarket ingredients and estimated per-serving macros are included.
  • Search covers recipe names and ingredients. Expanded filters include preparation time, family meals, protein, dietary needs and cuisines.
  • Any cal can be unticked; the filter row advertises horizontal scrolling.
  • Swaps rank by meal type, a calorie buffer and similar meal features. Lunch and dinner are compatible; unsuitable matches show an explanation.
  • Protein swaps offer matching dish families where a variant exists. Confirmation is followed by the calorie and macro difference.
  • Search Enter/Go blurs the input. Grams remain the default instead of ounces.
02

Food tracking that saves

A clear submit button, familiar portion units and a reusable meal workflow.

  • Food entry has a visible navy save action.
  • Bread supports slices, with equivalent grams and calories; other foods use natural portions where available.
  • After saving, add another ingredient or save the combination as a named meal.
  • The synthetic account saved Everyday toast and read it back from Recipes using the real food-library domain module.
03

Challenge, habits and navigation

One set of habits across Home and Challenge, clearer challenge cards and the correct category board.

  • Home and Challenge use the same challenge habits and editing route. The daily steps goal follows onboarding, including the tested 6,000-step target.
  • The duplicate legacy steps task is suppressed, and one workout row remains.
  • Onboarding replaces default habits and schedules the daily steps habit consistently.
  • Weekly and secret challenges have distinct presentation. The character fallback now renders an actual avatar.
  • Build and Tone maps to Tone. The category board and overall board both include eligible clients.
  • Shared Back controls restore the preceding app route and scroll position. Home/Challenge foreground restoration has the requested ten-minute window and honours deliberate section links.
04

The map and trainer challenges

Trainer-led tasks, private hidden locations and clearer discovery feedback.

  • Map cheers create recipient activity notices.
  • The trainer avatar anchors a tappable trainer challenge. Daniel’s broad jump accepts a measured result; Emma’s challenge records stair-master steps in three minutes.
  • Other trainers receive a short generic challenge until their own is configured.
  • Hidden challenge coordinates and instructions are withheld before discovery.
  • Search works at different zoom levels with warm, warmer and colder feedback. Gym-specific landmarks expand the clues.
  • The synthetic broad-jump result was saved at 180 cm. Tests cover result isolation, duplicates and integer stair-step counts.
05

Friends and club community

Visible activity alerts, messages between accepted friends and automatic club membership.

  • Challenge shows friend requests, acceptances, workout-sharing activity and cheers in a community card with a red unread badge.
  • Accepted friends can message each other.
  • Club membership is synchronised into club group chat for eligible Kickstart and Challenge clients.
  • Clients without a trainer choose their closest club during onboarding. The copy explains the community without mentioning leaving.
  • Clients can leave the club chat later. Membership synchronisation respects that choice.
  • Backend and bridge tests cover membership, personal-message access and community notices.
06

Check-ins and weight corrections

Correct record ownership and durable saves replace misleading sync failures.

  • Check-ins resolve the correct self-training identity and save the answers.
  • Weight editing targets the exact record, retains the corrected value and supports a second edit.
  • Focused tests reject wrong-owner and stale updates and prevent duplicate weekly check-ins.
07

Onboarding and program building

Starting measures are easy to defer, and uncertain injuries receive a clear explanation.

  • Add starting measures later is back at the top.
  • ACL and other common injury terms trigger targeted questions and conservative trainer review instead of silent failure.
  • Injury guidance covers common knee, shoulder, back, ankle and other conditions. This is a review aid, not automatic medical clearance.
  • My Training is available as a source when a trainer imports a program into a client account.
08

Trainer activity and booking

Less setup noise and a booking panel that opens without waiting for a preference save.

  • Trainer activity groups setup into meaningful onboarding-complete or needs-help events, with complete sentences.
  • The booking panel opens immediately while the preference write finishes separately. Preconnect hints prepare booking origins and a loading message explains the wait.
  • Trainer activity and My Training import were checked in code and relevant domain tests; their full trainer UI flows were not visually verified.
  • Booking-provider availability latency remains external. No real booking was submitted, and no measured provider-speed improvement is claimed.
09

JFkart controls

The Play button can be reached above the persistent bottom navigation.

  • The game page scrolls and reserves enough clearance for its controls.
  • Play visibility was inspected at 320 px and 390 px mobile widths.
